

When an observation appears to support a belief, there may be more than one explanation for it.

An Averist should consider the reasonable alternatives before settling on one.

This is especially important when an explanation involves something unusual. If an event can be explained by several possibilities, the fact that one of those possibilities exists does not make it the correct one.

For example, a person might become sick and then recover after performing a particular ritual. The recovery happened. The ritual may have mattered, but there are other possibilities. The illness may have run its normal course, another treatment may have helped, or the original diagnosis may have been wrong.

The point is not that the ritual definitely did nothing.

The point is that the observation alone does not tell us which explanation is correct.

A good explanation should account for the evidence better than the alternatives.

An Averist should therefore ask:

**What else could explain this?**

This question should be asked even when the preferred explanation feels obvious.

Alternative explanations should be taken seriously when they are supported by evidence. There is no value in inventing absurd possibilities simply to avoid a conclusion.

The aim is to compare plausible explanations fairly.

Sometimes the available evidence will strongly favour one explanation.

Sometimes several explanations will remain possible.

Sometimes the evidence will be too weak to choose between them.

In all three cases, the conclusion should reflect the actual situation.

An Averist should also remember that explanations can be combined. Two possible causes may both have contributed to an event. Finding one plausible explanation does not automatically eliminate the others.

When an important conclusion depends on one particular explanation, the evidence for that explanation should be examined separately from the evidence for the observation itself.

Seeing something happen is one question.

Determining why it happened is another.

**Before accepting an explanation, ask what else could account for the same evidence.**
