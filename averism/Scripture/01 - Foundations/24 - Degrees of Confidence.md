

Not every belief deserves the same level of confidence.

An Averist should try to match their confidence to the strength of the evidence.

A person might be almost certain that the Sun exists. They might be fairly confident about what they will eat for dinner. They might have a tentative opinion about why someone behaved a certain way.

These beliefs should not all be treated as equally certain.

Averism uses levels of confidence to make this difference clear.

**Certain** means that there is no reasonable way for the claim to be false given the definitions or evidence involved.

**Very high confidence** means the evidence is extremely strong, although a small possibility of error remains.

**High confidence** means the evidence strongly supports the claim and competing explanations are weaker.

**Moderate confidence** means the claim currently appears more likely than its alternatives, but important uncertainty remains.

**Low confidence** means there is some evidence supporting the claim, but there are serious reasons to doubt it.

**Very low confidence** means the claim is currently poorly supported or has strong evidence against it.

**Unknown** means there is not enough reliable information to make a reasonable judgement.

These labels are not measurements of truth itself. They describe how confident we should currently be.

Averists should also avoid pretending that precision exists when it does not.

Giving a belief a confidence of 63% does not automatically make it more rigorous than saying “moderate confidence.” A number should only be used when there is a reasonable method for producing it.

Confidence should change when the evidence changes.

Strong evidence can increase confidence. Strong counterevidence can reduce it. New information can do either.

Two people can also reasonably have different confidence levels if they have different information or assess the evidence differently.

Their disagreement should then focus on those differences.

Confidence is therefore something that can move.

A belief that begins with low confidence may become well supported.

A belief that begins with high confidence may later be weakened.

Averism does not require certainty before action.

It requires honesty about how certain we actually are.

**Do not give a belief more confidence than its reasons deserve.**
