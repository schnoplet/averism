

Some claims are much more difficult to accept than others.

A claim that fits well with established knowledge may need only ordinary evidence. A claim that would require us to reject a large amount of well-supported knowledge needs much stronger evidence.

For example, there is a large difference between claiming that someone was in a particular room and claiming that the person was able to survive without oxygen for an hour.

The second claim would require evidence that is much stronger because it conflicts with what we already have strong reason to believe about how human bodies work.

This does not mean that unusual claims are automatically false.

Science and history contain many examples of ideas that seemed unlikely before enough evidence was available. A claim should not be rejected merely because it is unusual.

The point is that the amount of evidence required should reflect how much the claim asks us to accept.

An extraordinary claim should therefore have evidence that is:

- reliable
    
- independently checkable
    
- specific enough to distinguish it from ordinary explanations
    
- strong enough to justify changing our existing beliefs
    

Averists should also be careful with explanations that require many separate assumptions.

If an ordinary explanation accounts for an event well, adding a second explanation should require a reason. More complicated explanations are sometimes correct, but complexity by itself is not evidence.

The same standard applies to supernatural claims.

Averism does not declare supernatural events impossible simply because they are supernatural. It asks what evidence would distinguish a supernatural explanation from a natural one.

If a person claims that an event was caused by a supernatural being, it is reasonable to ask why that explanation is better than the alternatives.

If no available evidence can distinguish the supernatural explanation from the ordinary ones, confidence in the supernatural explanation should remain limited.

Unusual claims can be true.

They simply require enough evidence to justify believing them.

**The stranger the claim, the more carefully its evidence should be examined.**
