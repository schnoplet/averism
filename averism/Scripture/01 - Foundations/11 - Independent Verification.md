

A claim becomes more reliable when other people can examine the evidence and reach the same result independently.

Averism therefore values independent verification.

If one person reports an unusual event, that report may be worth considering. If several independent people observe the same event and describe it consistently, the evidence becomes stronger.

Independence matters.

Ten people repeating the same story are not necessarily ten independent sources if they all received the story from the same original person.

The same problem can occur with written sources. Ten websites repeating one unsourced claim do not provide the same support as ten independent sources that reached the claim separately.

Whenever possible, important claims should therefore be checked against evidence that does not depend entirely on the same source.

Experiments should also be repeatable when the subject allows it. If a result appears once but cannot be reproduced under the same conditions, confidence in the result should be lower than it would be if other people could repeatedly obtain it.

Reproduction does not guarantee that a claim is true. A repeated mistake is still a mistake. It does, however, make certain kinds of error easier to detect and investigate.

Independent verification is especially important for extraordinary claims.

The more a claim conflicts with well-established knowledge, the stronger the evidence should generally be before it is accepted.

Averists should also be willing to let other people examine their own reasoning.

A belief that only survives when nobody is allowed to question its evidence is a weak belief.

Good evidence should remain good when someone else checks it.

**Whenever possible, do not rely on one person's word when the claim can be independently checked.**
