

Not all evidence deserves the same weight.

Averism therefore uses a hierarchy when comparing different kinds of evidence. This does not mean that one type of evidence is always correct or that another type is always useless. It means that some methods are generally better at producing reliable information than others.

Evidence that can be independently observed, measured, repeated, and checked will usually deserve more weight than evidence that depends entirely on memory or personal interpretation.

For example, a result that can be reproduced by many independent researchers is generally stronger than a single person's recollection of an unusual event.

A detailed written record can be useful evidence about what a person believed or what they reported at a certain time. It does not automatically prove that the events described actually happened.

Personal experience can also be important. A person's experience is real as an experience, but the explanation they give for it may still be wrong.

Averists should therefore separate:

**What was observed?**

**What was reported?**

**What was inferred from it?**

These are different things.

Evidence can also be strengthened when different methods reach the same conclusion independently. For example, a historical event supported by documents, physical evidence, and independent contemporary accounts is generally more convincing than a claim supported by one source alone.

The hierarchy should never be used mechanically.

A weak source can sometimes provide the correct answer. A strong method can also produce a bad result if it is used incorrectly.

The purpose of an evidence hierarchy is to help decide where confidence should be placed when evidence conflicts.

When two pieces of evidence disagree, an Averist should examine why.

One source may have better records. One measurement may have greater accuracy. One account may have been produced closer to the event. One experiment may have been repeated while the other has not.

The conflict should be investigated rather than resolved by choosing whichever piece of evidence is more convenient.

Evidence is strongest when it can survive careful checking from people who have no reason to make it say what they want.

**Good evidence is evidence that remains useful after it has been questioned.**
