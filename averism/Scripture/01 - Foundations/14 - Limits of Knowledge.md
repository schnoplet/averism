

Human knowledge has limits.

There are things we can observe directly, things we can work out from evidence, and things we may not currently have any reliable way to know.

Averism takes these limits seriously.

A person should not assume that a question has an answer simply because it is possible to ask it. Some questions may require information that humans do not have. Others may be impossible to settle with the tools available to us.

This does not mean that every unknown question is equally mysterious.

There is a difference between a question that is currently unanswered and a question for which there is no known way to obtain an answer. There is also a difference between an unanswered question and a claim that has already been tested and found to be poorly supported.

When knowledge is limited, people often fill the gap with assumptions. Sometimes those assumptions are useful. They become a problem when they are treated as established facts.

An Averist should keep track of where knowledge ends and speculation begins.

This applies to Averism itself.

If Averism reaches a question that cannot currently be answered, the religion should allow the question to remain open. A missing answer should not be replaced with a confident statement simply to make the system feel complete.

The limits of knowledge can also change.

Something that cannot be investigated today may become testable later because of new technology, new discoveries, or a better understanding of the problem.

For that reason, “we do not know” does not have to mean “we can never know.”

It means that, at the present time, we do not have enough reason to give a reliable answer.

An Averist should be willing to live with that uncertainty.

**Do not turn an unanswered question into an answer just because leaving it unanswered feels uncomfortable.**
