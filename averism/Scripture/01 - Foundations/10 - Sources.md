

Averism depends on people being able to check where a claim came from.

A source is any material used as evidence for a claim. It could be a scientific paper, a historical document, a record of an event, a person's testimony, a measurement, or another form of information.

Sources differ in reliability.

A source should therefore be examined rather than accepted simply because it exists.

When possible, an Averist should consider who produced the source, when it was produced, how the information was obtained, whether the source could have been mistaken or deliberately misleading, and whether independent sources support it.

A source should also be used for what it can actually tell us.

A historical document can provide evidence that someone wrote something at a particular time. It does not automatically prove that everything the document claims is true.

Likewise, a scientific study can provide evidence for a particular result under particular conditions. Its existence does not prove every conclusion someone later attaches to it.

Primary sources can be especially useful when investigating an event, but they are not automatically correct. A person who witnessed an event can still misunderstand what happened.

Secondary sources can also be valuable because they may compare many pieces of evidence and provide context. They should still be traceable to the evidence they are discussing.

When an important Averist claim depends on a source, the source should be recorded so that another person can examine it for themselves.

Whenever practical, Averism should prefer sources that are accessible, independently checkable, and clear about how their information was obtained.

If an important claim currently depends on a source that cannot be examined, this limitation should be recorded.

Averism should never hide the origin of its evidence.

A person should be able to ask:

**“How do we know this?”**

and find a path back to the evidence.
