

Averism needs a method that can be used in practice.

When an important question comes up, an Averist should first identify exactly what is being claimed. Vague claims are difficult to examine because they can change meaning without anyone noticing.

Next, the Averist should gather the relevant evidence.

The evidence should then be checked for reliability. Sources should be examined, assumptions should be identified, and alternative explanations should be considered.

After that, the Averist should form a conclusion based on the available evidence.

The conclusion should include an appropriate level of confidence. A person should not describe something as certain when the evidence only makes it likely.

The final step is to remain open to revision.

This process does not guarantee a correct answer. It reduces some of the ways people commonly arrive at bad answers.

An Averist can still make mistakes. They can misunderstand evidence, miss an important alternative, use a poor source, or make an error in reasoning.

The method is meant to make those mistakes easier to find.

When two Averists reach different conclusions, they should be able to compare their reasoning.

They should be able to identify where they disagree about the evidence, the reliability of a source, an assumption, a value, or the reasoning connecting the premises to the conclusion.

This makes disagreement useful.

The goal is not for every Averist to reach the same answer immediately.

The goal is for conclusions to have clear reasons behind them and for those reasons to remain open to examination.

The method can be summarised as:

**Define the claim.**

**Gather the evidence.**

**Check the evidence.**

**Consider alternatives.**

**Reach a conclusion.**

**State your confidence.**

**Remain open to correction.**

This method applies to Averism itself.

If the method leads to a conclusion that conflicts with an existing Averist teaching, the conflict should be investigated rather than ignored.

Averism begins with the search for truth.

Its own beliefs are included in that search.
