

A person should be free to examine an idea.

This includes the ideas of Averism itself.

Averism should not require a person to accept a teaching before they are allowed to question it. People should be able to read the Canon, examine its arguments, compare them with other ideas, and reach their own conclusions.

A person should also be free to leave Averism.

Leaving the religion does not make someone a bad person. It does not make everything they previously believed worthless, and it should not make them a target of punishment or intimidation.

The same freedom should apply inside the religion.

An Averist should be able to disagree with a leader, challenge an established interpretation, or argue that a teaching should be changed. They should be able to do this without being pressured to pretend that they agree.

Freedom does not mean that every idea must be accepted as true.

A person can be criticised. An argument can be rejected. A claim can be shown to have poor evidence.

The important distinction is between **challenging an idea** and **controlling the person who holds it**.

Averism should not use threats, isolation, or punishment to force agreement with its teachings.

A person who changes their beliefs should be allowed to do so openly.

A person who remains uncertain should be allowed to remain uncertain.

A person who rejects Averism should be allowed to say so.

This principle also protects Averism from becoming dependent on obedience.

If people are only allowed to agree, the religion loses one of the things it depends on most: honest examination.

A belief that survives because people are free to question it has a stronger basis than one protected by fear.

**No belief deserves protection from examination simply because it is part of Averism.**
