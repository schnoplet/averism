
This is the beginning of Averism.

Averism was created around one basic idea: **we should try to believe what is true, and we should care whether we are wrong.**

The word _Aver_ refers to the process of getting closer to what is actually true through honest reasoning, evidence, and examination. An Averist is not expected to have every answer. In many cases, the correct answer may simply be that we do not know yet.

Averism exists because people have always had questions that are difficult to answer. Why does anything exist? Why are we conscious? How should we live? Does life have a purpose? Is there something beyond the physical world? What happens when we die?

This religion does not begin by assuming that the answers are already known.

Instead, it starts with the belief that a claim should have a reason behind it. A belief can be based on evidence, reasoning, experience, tradition, or some combination of these, but those sources are not equally reliable and should be treated accordingly.

Being confident does not make something true. Neither does being old, popular, comforting, or written in a religious text.

Averism also takes the possibility of error seriously. If an Averist discovers that one of their beliefs is poorly supported or false, changing that belief is considered a good thing. The purpose of the religion is not to make its followers permanently agree with its original ideas.

Some parts of Averism may eventually become very well supported. Others may remain uncertain. Some may be rejected completely. The distinction between these cases matters.

The scripture therefore records ideas, arguments, questions, and conclusions according to how well supported they are. Disagreement is allowed. Criticism is allowed. New evidence can change a conclusion.

Averism does not promise that every important question has an answer.

It does promise that those questions are worth taking seriously.

This is the standard that the rest of the religion is built on.
