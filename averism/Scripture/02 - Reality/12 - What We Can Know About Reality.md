

We do not experience all of reality directly.

We experience the world through our senses, our bodies, our brains, and the tools we create. Each of these gives us information, but each also has limits.

This means there will always be a difference between reality itself and our understanding of it.

That difference does not make knowledge impossible.

We can compare observations, test predictions, repeat measurements, examine competing explanations, and correct errors. Over time, these methods can give us increasingly reliable descriptions of the world.

Some things are well established.

Other things are uncertain.

Some questions may remain unanswered for a very long time.

Averism should keep these categories separate.

A belief should not become certain simply because it is part of Averism. A question should not be called impossible simply because we do not currently know how to answer it. A possibility should not become a fact merely because nobody has disproved it.

This also means that Averism has to accept uncomfortable conclusions.

If the evidence points toward something that conflicts with an existing Averist belief, the belief has to be reconsidered.

If the evidence is not enough, the answer should remain uncertain.

If two explanations fit the evidence equally well, Averism should not pretend that one has been proven.

Our understanding of reality is therefore always subject to correction.

That is not a weakness in the search for knowledge. It is one of the conditions that makes the search honest.

Reality exists whether or not we understand it.

Our beliefs are attempts to describe it.

Those attempts can improve.

They can also fail.

Averism accepts both possibilities.

**We do not need complete knowledge to seek truth. We only need to be honest about what we know, what we infer, and what we do not know.**

This ends the foundations of Averism's view of reality.

The next part turns inward, toward the person doing the investigating.

**What is a self? What is consciousness? Do we have free will? What makes someone the same person over time?**

Those questions begin the study of the self.
