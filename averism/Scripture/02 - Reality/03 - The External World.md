

We experience a world that appears to exist outside our minds.

We see objects, hear sounds, feel surfaces, and observe events that we do not seem to control. Other people also report experiencing the same world.

This gives us strong reason to believe that something exists outside our individual minds.

There is still a question about exactly what that world is like.

Our senses are limited. Human eyes cannot see every form of light. Human ears cannot hear every sound. Our brains also interpret the information they receive rather than simply displaying reality exactly as it is.

Because of this, perception should be treated as evidence rather than perfect access to reality.

The strongest reason to believe in an external world comes from the consistency of our experiences.

Objects usually remain where we leave them. Other people can independently observe the same places and events. Predictions based on our understanding of the physical world often work.

These patterns would be difficult to explain if our experiences had no relationship at all to something outside our minds.

This does not completely settle the philosophical question.

It is possible to imagine scenarios in which our experiences are being produced by something other than an ordinary physical world. The existence of such a possibility does not mean that we have good reason to believe one of those scenarios is true.

Averism therefore accepts the external world as the best-supported explanation of our shared experience, while recognising that our knowledge of it is indirect.

We should expect our understanding of reality to improve as we find better ways to observe and test it.

The world does not need to look exactly the way our minds first imagined it.

Our models are supposed to describe reality.

Reality is not supposed to fit our models.

**The world we experience gives us strong evidence for an external reality, even though our access to that reality is imperfect.**
