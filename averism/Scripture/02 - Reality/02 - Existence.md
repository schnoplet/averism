

Something exists.

This is the first fact that Averism can begin with.

At the very least, there is an experience happening right now. We may question what caused that experience, what kind of thing is experiencing it, and whether our perception of the outside world is accurate.

But we cannot honestly claim that absolutely nothing exists while there is an experience of making that claim.

From this starting point, several possibilities remain open.

There may be a physical universe that exists independently of minds. There may be aspects of reality that we do not currently understand. There may be forms of existence that humans have not discovered. There may also be limits to what we are capable of knowing.

Averism does not choose between these possibilities without sufficient reason.

The fact that something exists does not by itself tell us what that thing is.

It does not prove that the universe had a beginning. It does not prove that the universe has no beginning. It does not prove that a god exists. It does not prove that no god exists.

Those are separate questions.

This distinction matters because it is easy to take one basic fact and build conclusions onto it that the original fact does not support.

Averism starts with what can be established and moves outward from there.

The existence of something is certain from the fact that something is being experienced, but the nature of that existence remains an open question.

**Something exists. What it is must be investigated rather than assumed.**
