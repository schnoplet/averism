

There is a difference between something being possible and something being necessary.

Something is **possible** if it could be true.

Something is **necessary** if it must be true.

These ideas are useful when thinking about reality because people often move from “this could be true” to “this must be true” without noticing.

For example, it may be possible that life exists somewhere else in the universe. That does not mean that life must exist elsewhere.

It may also be possible that the universe could have been different. That does not tell us whether the universe actually could have been different or what would have caused those differences.

Averism therefore treats possibility as a starting point for investigation, not as evidence by itself.

Almost any idea can be imagined.

Imagining something does not show that it exists, and it does not show that it is likely to exist.

Necessity requires a stronger argument.

To say that something must exist, or that something could not possibly have been different, requires a reason that rules out the alternatives.

This is especially important in arguments about God, the universe, mathematics, and the basic structure of reality.

An argument may show that something is possible without showing that it is actual.

It may show that something is unlikely without showing that it is impossible.

It may show that something follows from a particular set of assumptions without showing that those assumptions are true.

These distinctions should remain clear.

Averism also recognises that our current understanding of what is possible may be incomplete. Something that seems impossible based on current knowledge may turn out to be possible once we understand more.

That does not mean that every impossible-seeming idea should be believed.

It means that **“we cannot currently explain this”** and **“this cannot happen”** are different claims.

The same caution applies to necessity.

Before saying that something must be true, an Averist should ask what would happen if it were false.

If a coherent alternative exists, then necessity has not been established.

Possibility can open a question.

Evidence and reasoning are what help answer it.

**Possible does not mean probable, and probable does not mean necessary.**
