

Reality is whatever exists independently of what we believe about it.

A person can be mistaken about reality, but their mistake does not change what is actually there.

If a mountain exists and nobody knows about it, the mountain still exists.

If everyone believes the mountain is somewhere else, it does not move because of their belief.

This seems obvious when talking about ordinary objects. It becomes harder when asking what reality itself is.

We experience the world through our senses, but our senses do not give us a perfect picture of everything that exists. They give us information that our brains process.

We can already see this in ordinary life. People can mishear sounds, misread words, see things that are not there, or remember events incorrectly.

Because of this, an Averist should distinguish between **experience** and **reality**.

An experience tells us that something appeared a certain way to us.

It does not automatically tell us why it appeared that way or whether our interpretation is correct.

This does not mean that our senses are useless. Quite the opposite. Most of what we know about the world depends on them in some way. The important point is that sensory information is evidence about reality, not reality itself.

Reality also does not have to match human intuition.

The universe may contain things that seem strange, counterintuitive, or impossible based on everyday experience. When evidence repeatedly shows that reality behaves differently from what we expected, our expectations should change.

Averism therefore begins with a basic assumption:

**There is a reality that does not depend on what humans believe about it.**

Our job is to understand that reality as accurately as we can.

We may never understand all of it.

That does not make the attempt pointless.

It means there is something real to investigate.
