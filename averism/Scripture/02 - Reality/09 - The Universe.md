

The universe is everything that exists as part of physical reality, as far as we currently understand it.

We know that the observable universe is extremely large and contains vast numbers of galaxies, stars, planets, and other objects. We also know that the universe has changed over time.

The current scientific model describes an expanding universe that was once in a much hotter and denser state. This model explains a large amount of observed evidence, including the expansion of distant galaxies and the cosmic microwave background.

It does not answer every question about the universe.

For example, the model does not by itself explain why there is a universe rather than nothing, whether anything existed before the earliest state we can describe, or whether our universe is the only one.

These questions go beyond what current evidence can establish.

Averism should therefore distinguish between the parts of cosmology that have strong observational support and the ideas proposed to explain what lies beyond current observations.

A scientific model can be extremely successful without being a complete description of reality.

This has happened before. Older scientific models were often useful within their limits and were later replaced or expanded when better evidence became available.

That does not make previous knowledge worthless. It shows that our understanding can improve.

The same principle applies to the universe.

Averism should accept well-supported scientific conclusions while remaining open to their refinement or replacement when better evidence appears.

The universe should not be forced to fit a religious explanation.

If evidence eventually shows that a current Averist belief about the universe is wrong, the belief should change.

Reality has no obligation to agree with us.

**Our understanding of the universe should follow the evidence, wherever that evidence leads.**
