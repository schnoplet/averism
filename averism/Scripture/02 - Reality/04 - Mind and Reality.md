

A person's experience of reality includes more than what they can observe outside themselves.

We also experience thoughts, memories, emotions, sensations, and awareness.

These experiences are part of what we call the mind.

The existence of a mind is difficult to deny because the act of thinking is itself an experience. What is less certain is what a mind actually is.

One possibility is that consciousness is produced entirely by physical processes in the brain.

Another possibility is that consciousness involves something that current physical explanations do not fully describe.

There may be other possibilities as well.

Averism should not settle this question simply because one explanation feels more intuitive.

There is substantial evidence that changes to the brain can change a person's thoughts, memories, personality, perception, and behaviour. This gives strong support to the idea that the brain is closely connected to consciousness.

It does not by itself answer every question about why conscious experience exists.

There is a difference between explaining the functions of the brain and explaining why those functions are accompanied by subjective experience at all.

This is one of the questions that Averism leaves open for investigation.

The existence of unanswered questions should not be treated as evidence for a particular explanation.

Not knowing how something works does not establish what caused it.

At the same time, an incomplete explanation should not be treated as a complete one simply because it is currently the best available explanation.

Averism therefore accepts two ideas at once: our current understanding of the mind has strong evidence behind parts of it, and important questions about consciousness remain unresolved.

Those questions should remain open until better evidence or reasoning gives us a stronger conclusion.

**The mind is real as an experience. What the mind ultimately is remains a question to investigate.**
