

We experience events as happening in a sequence.

We remember things that happened before now and can make plans for things that have not happened yet. Clocks allow us to compare the duration between events.

Time therefore plays an important role in how we understand change.

What time actually is is a deeper question.

In ordinary experience, time seems to move from the past toward the future. Modern physics gives us a more complicated picture. Measurements of time can depend on motion and gravity, and there is no single universal clock that works in exactly the same way for every observer.

This does not mean that time is unreal.

It means that our everyday idea of time is incomplete.

There is also a difference between saying that time exists and saying that time must have had a beginning.

Those are separate claims.

If the universe had a beginning, we can ask what caused it or whether the word _before_ even makes sense when talking about the beginning of time itself.

If the universe has existed forever, we can ask how an infinite past relates to the events we experience now.

Averism should not assume that one answer is correct simply because it seems easier to imagine.

Our intuition about time was developed for ordinary human life. It was not designed to answer questions about the whole universe.

Averism therefore treats time as a real feature of our experience and of the physical models that successfully describe observations, while leaving deeper questions about its ultimate nature open.

**We experience time and can measure its effects. What time ultimately is remains a question of reality, not intuition.**
