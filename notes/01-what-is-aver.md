---
title: "01 - What is Aver"
---


The word _Aver_ is at the centre of Averism.

An Aver is an attempt to determine what is true while remaining willing to discover that you are wrong.

It is more than simply having an opinion. An opinion can be held because it feels right, because someone you trust said it, or because it is what you have always believed. An Aver requires a reason for the belief and a willingness to examine that reason.

An Aver can be about anything.

A person can make an Aver about the physical world, another person, morality, consciousness, history, or the existence of something beyond what we can currently observe.

An Aver does not have to produce a final answer. Sometimes the most honest result is that the evidence is insufficient.

This is important because there is a difference between **what is true** and **what we have reason to believe is true**.

Reality does not change because we misunderstand it. Our beliefs do.

Averism is concerned with that gap.

The purpose of an Aver is to make that gap smaller.

When forming an Aver, an Averist should consider the available evidence, the quality of the reasoning, possible alternative explanations, and reasons the conclusion might be wrong.

An Aver can also be revised.

If better evidence appears, or if an important mistake is found in the reasoning, the Aver should change. This is not treated as a failure. It means the process worked.

There is therefore no rule that says every Averist must reach the same conclusion about every question.

The important part is the method used to reach the conclusion.

**An Aver is a belief held with reasons and kept open to correction.**
