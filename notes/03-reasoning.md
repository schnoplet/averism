---
title: "03 - Reasoning"
---



Evidence gives us information. Reasoning is how we use that information to reach a conclusion.

Averism treats good reasoning as necessary because the same evidence can be interpreted in very different ways.

A conclusion should follow from the reasons given for it. If the reasons do not support the conclusion, the conclusion should not be accepted simply because it sounds convincing.

Averists should be careful about assumptions. An argument can appear strong while depending on something that has never been established.

For example, seeing that two events happened together does not by itself show that one caused the other. A person getting better after taking a particular treatment does not prove that the treatment caused the improvement. Other factors may have been involved.

The same applies to patterns. Humans are very good at noticing patterns, including patterns that happened by chance. A pattern should therefore be tested before it is treated as meaningful.

Reasoning should also be consistent. If two arguments use the same principles, those principles should not suddenly change because the conclusion is inconvenient.

Averists should distinguish between what is possible and what is likely.

Something can be possible without being a good explanation of what happened. Almost anything can be imagined. That does not make every explanation equally reasonable.

When several explanations fit the available evidence, an Averist should compare them rather than immediately choosing the one they prefer.

A good explanation should account for the evidence without requiring unnecessary assumptions.

Reasoning also has limits. Some questions cannot currently be answered because the necessary evidence does not exist. In those cases, careful reasoning can show what follows from what we already know, but it cannot manufacture missing information.

An Averist should therefore be comfortable saying:

**“The evidence does not allow us to conclude that yet.”**

Being able to reach a conclusion is useful.

Knowing when a conclusion has not been earned is just as important.
