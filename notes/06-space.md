---
title: "06 - Space"
---



Things appear to exist somewhere.

We describe objects as having positions and distances, and we can measure those distances with increasing precision.

Like time, space seems obvious in everyday life but becomes less straightforward when considered at the scale of the universe.

Modern physics describes space and time as closely connected. Matter and energy affect the geometry of spacetime, and that geometry affects how matter and light move.

This tells us something important about reality: the way space behaves does not have to match our ordinary intuition.

There is also a deeper question about what space itself is.

Does space exist independently, like a container in which everything else sits? Is space a property of relationships between things? Could space emerge from something more fundamental?

Averism does not currently have enough reason to settle these questions as religious doctrine.

The same caution applies to the size and shape of the universe.

The part of the universe we can observe is limited by the speed of light and the age of the universe. There may be parts of reality that we cannot currently observe directly.

The observable universe should therefore not automatically be treated as the whole of reality.

What we can observe gives us evidence about the larger universe, but there is a difference between an observation and the conclusion that nothing exists beyond what we can currently detect.

Averism accepts that space has measurable physical properties and that our best scientific theories can describe many of them successfully.

Questions about the ultimate nature, extent, and origin of space remain open where the evidence does not yet provide a reliable answer.

**We can measure where things are. That does not mean we already understand what space itself is.**
